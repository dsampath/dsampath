# Hi, I like building things.

I’m a builder and a tinkerer. I like going deep, all the way from infrastructure to applications, and I enjoy the craft of making good products that people actually use. The details matter: how something works underneath, how it feels in your hands, and whether you want to come back to it tomorrow.

For me, the best starting point is often something I want to use myself. Build a small version, live with it, find the rough edges, and keep making it better. Sometimes it stays a personal experiment. Sometimes it becomes useful to other people too.

Agentic coding makes this an especially exciting time to build. More people can take an idea, make something real, and try it for themselves. I think it’s important to keep doing that: test the tools, question the results, learn where they help, and develop a feel for what makes software worth using. The ability to generate code is powerful; the craft grows by building, using, and improving the product.

This is my small attempt at building software, learning in the open, and sharing a few starting points along the way.

## AI Defense

### [DefenseClaw](https://github.com/cisco-ai-defense/defenseclaw)

Security governance for AI coding agents and agentic runtimes, under Cisco AI Defense.

I set up the initial project and checked in V1 of the terminal user interface (TUI). That was an early starting point. The team has built on it, improved it, and taken the project much further over time. It’s been great to see that first version grow through the work of many people.

## Personal assistants

### [Keel](https://github.com/Keel-Labs/keel)

A local-first AI assistant we built together around the idea of a Chief of Staff on the Mac, with your context and memories in files you own.

Built with **Medha Ghatikesh**, who did the majority of the work. I contributed some revisions along the way. This is very much her craft and effort, with a little tinkering from me.

## Experiments you can build on

### [TV App Starter](https://github.com/dsampath/tv-app-starter)

I wanted to build apps for my Apple TV and Android TV, so I tried doing it with Codex. The experience was fun enough to turn into a reusable starting point for other people.

Two native sample apps, a Codex skill, and a Claude Code plugin. Bring your own idea, get it on a screen, and make something you’ll enjoy using. [Take it for a spin →](https://github.com/dsampath/tv-app-starter#readme)
